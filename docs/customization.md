# Customization notes

Findings from going through the bought art and building a lab to try it,
October 2026: what the packs hold, what an item, a layout and a character
need to be, what went wrong when we tried, and an order to build it in.
Today an office is one fixed floor and eight premade characters; the packs
hold enough for people to dress themselves, decorate their desks, and admins
to furnish and theme their offices.

## What the packs hold

**Modern Interiors** (LimeZu, full version), at 16, 32 and 48px. We work at 32.

- **26 themes** of furniture and objects, each as a sheet and as cut-out
  single images: about 5,300 singles at 32px in each shadow style (black
  shadow, which we use, plain, shadowless). Generic, living room, bathroom,
  bedroom, classroom and library, music and sport, art, gym, fishing,
  birthday party, Halloween, kitchen, conference hall, basement, Christmas,
  grocery store, an upstairs system, jail, hospital, Japanese interiors,
  clothing store, museum, TV studio, ice-cream shop, shooting range,
  condominium. Singles are trimmed to a 16px grid and **numbered, not named**,
  so a catalog has to name them.
- **Room builder**: about 75 floor materials and 55 wall styles, in the same
  corner, edge and fill layout our map already uses, plus baseboards, borders,
  floor paths, arched entryways and 3D walls. Swapping materials is enough to
  re-theme a room.
- **621 animated objects** (GIFs and sprite sheets): doors that open (glass,
  sliding, elevator), coffee machines, fridges, server racks and control-room
  screens, security cameras, fish tanks, a cat, clocks, candles, fireplaces,
  party balloons, seasonal pieces.
- **14 premade room designs**, each in layers below and above the characters.
- **UI elements**, including animated "thinking" emotes.

**The character generator** in the same pack: 9 bodies (6 natural skin tones,
3 fantasy), 7 eye colours, 33 outfits in 3 to 10 colours (132 sheets), 29
hairstyles in about 7 colours (200), 19 accessories (glasses, beard,
moustache, beanie, caps, hats, mask, backpack and some costume pieces), plus
children's versions, phones and books, and 20 premade characters. Every sheet
is on the same grid of 32×64 frames: row 1 idle and row 2 walk (six frames
each for right, up, left, down), rows 4 and 5 sitting (side views only), then
phone, reading, carrying and others.

**Modern Office Revamped**: the office sheet we use today (desks in four
finishes, L-desks, office chairs in four colours, monitors, laptops,
printers, copiers, sofas, plants, boards, vending machines) and 339 singles.
The first 95 office singles are desk *pieces*, not desks: whole desks exist
only on the sheet.

The free and RPG Maker versions add nothing we'd use.

**Licence.** Both packs may be used and edited in a commercial product,
not redistributed, with credit (already served in `credits.txt`). Showing the
art in an editor is use; letting people download sprites or sheets is not
something to offer.

## What we use today

One office floor, generated by `tools/buildmap.py` into a Tiled map, drawn
from the office sheet; eight legacy 16px characters at twice their size. Which
chairs are desks, and where you arrive for each, is hard-coded in
`shared-protocol/src/map.ts` by tile number. Anything customizable has to
replace both: the floor must come from data, and so must its seats.

## How the floor looks: the rules

Decided by looking, and checked by the lab on every change (`lab/check.mjs`):

- **Nobody sits sideways.** Seated side-on, a character reads badly, so a seat
  faces up (your back to us, at a desk or the far side of a table) or down.
  The packs' side chairs aren't in the catalog.
- **A desk is used from the front.** One person, one chair, in front of the
  desk, facing it. Desks back to back, with people on both sides, put the far
  person behind the desk, its monitors and the divider, and made what covers
  whom hard to get right; a one-sided desk has one answer.
- **A desk is the one the office has now**: wide (six tiles), with the glass
  divider and its posts behind it, in one of five finishes (maple, oak,
  graphite, white, beech). The packs' small three-tile desks looked cramped
  and aren't used.
- **A desk always has a chair.** A desk is a bundle of the desk and a chair
  slot. The chair can be swapped for another office chair (black, low black,
  tan) but never taken away, and the seat stays in the same spot whichever
  chair it is.
- **Monitors and the like face the camera.** With everyone at a desk facing
  up, what's on a desk shows its front; the backs of monitors aren't needed.

## Items

An item is everything the floor needs to know about one thing:

- **sprite**: a rectangle cut from a pack image, on the 16px grid, optionally
  mirrored; or layers cut from several places and drawn in order, for things
  the packs draw in pieces (a six-tile desk is its divider, three desk pieces
  with the middle repeated, and post caps, as the current map draws it);
- **mount**: on the floor, on a wall, or on a surface (a monitor on a desk);
- **colliders**: rectangles you can't walk into, in the sprite's pixels;
- **sort line**: above it you're behind the item, below it in front;
- **seats**: a point and which way you face sitting there (up or down);
- **surface**: where surface things can go (a desk's top);
- **part**: only ever placed inside a bundle (a desk top without its chair);
- name, category, themes.

A **bundle** is items placed and moved together, each part at an offset; a
part can be a slot with options (the chair). Desks and tables with their
chairs are bundles.

The first 55 (five desks, their desk tops, two meeting tables, chairs, sofas,
plants, kitchen and tech pieces) are in `shared-protocol/src/catalog/items.json`.
What making them taught us:

- **Every item needs looking at.** A first guess (a collider as the bottom
  third of the item's pixels, the sort line at its foot) is right for plants
  and cabinets and wrong for chairs, sofas and partitions.
- **A chair you sit in facing up needs its sort line at its very bottom**, so
  the backrest covers whoever sits there and their head shows over it.
- **Shadows join objects on a sheet.** Finding boxes automatically works for
  most things but returns a row of four chairs as one, so cutting takes an eye.
- **Office singles aren't desks.** The office pack's single images include
  desk pieces, not whole desks; the sheet, cut in layers, is the reliable source.
- **Our characters' side-sitting frames are swapped** (frame 49 faces right,
  50 left, against `SIT_ORDER` in `AnimationManager`). With no sideways seats
  it never shows; worth fixing so it never surprises anyone.

## Layouts

A layout is a shell (floors and walls) and a list of placed items and bundles,
each with a position on the 16px grid and, for a desk, its chosen chair. The
first, "Startup" (`shared-protocol/src/catalog/layouts/startup.json`),
furnishes today's walls: four rows of three maple desks, joined so neighbours
share a post, every chair in front; a meeting table with chairs above and
below; a break corner; a lounge.

What a real office needs from it:

- **Rooms as rectangles with materials.** A room is a rectangle with a floor
  and a wall style; the floor and wall tiles are worked out from the
  rectangles when the floor loads (the corner and edge logic in
  `buildmap.py`). A theme is then materials plus item variants.
- **Seats come from items.** A seat's id is the placed item and its seat's
  index, not a tile number. Where you arrive for a desk is worked out when the
  layout is saved, with the floor's own pathfinding, and saved with it, so the
  realtime server needs no pathfinding and checks desk claims against the
  layout's seats.
- **Saved per office, with versions.** An admin edits a draft and publishes
  it; while one person edits, no one else can, so two edits never cross.
  Everyone on the floor gets the new layout and redraws.
- **A limit.** A ceiling on items per floor keeps drawing and loading
  bounded on phones.
- **Templates.** A new office starts from a template chosen by size (how many
  people) and look (a theme), all designed in the layout editor from catalog
  items. Templates can be generated from a few room blocks (desk island,
  meeting room, lounge, break corner) arranged for a headcount, rather than
  drawn one by one.

## Themes

What suits work, from the packs: office, library and reading room, lounge
(living room), kitchen and break room, conference, Japanese (a calm, "zen"
office), art studio, a music and games corner, a gym. Seasonal sets
(Christmas, Halloween, birthday) suit limited-time decor. Animated objects
(a coffee machine that brews, doors that open, a fish tank, server lights)
make an office feel lived in, and fit the floor's quiet tone if used sparingly.

## Characters

- **Layers compose exactly.** Body, eyes, outfit, hair, accessory, drawn in
  that order, line up on every frame; nothing needs adjusting.
- **Sitting is drawn from the side only.** Facing up or down, a seated
  character is their standing pose, as our characters already are.
- **Sharper at the same size.** The 32px generator draws 32×64 frames at
  1×; on the floor they're the size of today's 16px characters at 2×, with
  twice the detail.
- **Cheap to load.** Cropped to the rows the floor uses (idle, walk, sit),
  the 432 part sheets come to 2.75 MB, about 6.5 KB each; a character is
  about 30 KB, and parts are shared and cached.
- **A recipe on the profile.** A character is five small choices
  (`{ body, eyes, outfit, hair, accessory }`); each client composes the
  sheets into one texture when someone arrives. The eight premade characters
  become presets.
- **Colours are swaps.** Colour variants change about six colours of a ramp
  and keep outlines and shading, so any colour could be made later by
  remapping the ramp.
- **For the editor:** each style shown on the person as they look now, the
  colours as swatches, a Randomize, and a preview walking, standing and
  sitting. Fantasy skins and costume pieces belong out of the default list.

## Desks

A claimed desk is the natural place to start customization, because it's
yours and it stays yours (`product.md`):

- your desk's surface takes surface items (a monitor, a lamp, a plant, a
  mug), up to a small number, all facing the camera;
- you choose its chair, from the office chairs; it always has one;
- the desk can change finish (the same desk in another wood);
- it's done on the floor, at the desk, not in a separate editor;
- what you put on it stays with the desk, which is why desks are claimed and
  not moved.

## The lab and the pipeline

`lab/` is a local tool, kept in the repository and never deployed (its
README says how to run it). Items, bundles, layouts and characters are made
there, and it is the only way art reaches the floor:

1. **Cut** an item from a pack's sheet or singles, or layer it from pieces.
2. **Fit** its colliders, sort line, seats and surface by dragging them on a
   floor while a character walks round it and sits in it; bundle it.
3. **Check**: the house rules run on every change in the lab and in
   `pnpm --dir lab check`, which needs no art and can run anywhere.
4. **Build**: `pnpm --dir lab build` bakes every drawn item into one atlas
   (50 items, 70 KB) in `private-assets/catalog/`, writes where each one is
   to `shared-protocol/src/catalog/atlas.json`, and records the atlas in the
   asset manifest.
5. **Upload** the atlas to R2 with the command the build prints; every build
   of the app then pulls and checks it like the rest of the art (`ASSETS.md`).

The catalog, bundles and layouts are our own data and live in git; the art
only ever leaves `assets-pack/` as the baked atlas, through R2.

## What depends on what

Each of these needs the one before it:

1. **The floor from data.** The atlas on the floor, an office drawn from a
   layout, seats from the layout instead of `map.ts`. The one visible change:
   one-sided desks.
2. **Characters.** The creator, the recipe on the profile, composing on the
   client; the premade eight as presets.
3. **Desk decor.** Surface items on your own desk, on the floor.
4. **Furnishing for admins.** Place, move and remove items; draft, publish,
   one editor at a time.
5. **Templates and themes.** New offices from templates by size and look;
   room materials; seasonal sets.
6. **Animated objects**, used sparingly.
